# Competition logos

Every competition in the game (64), from a save on 2028-01-15, and the logo it shows. Generated by
`scripts/assets/comp_inventory.ts`; checked on 2026-10-21 with full network access.

- **Bundled, verified** (54): retrieved from football-logos.cc at 700px, looked at against the real
  identity (current where a competition rebranded: the 2025 VriendenLoterij Eredivisie mark, the January 2026 Liga
  Profesional mark), trimmed to the mark itself and bundled at a 360px long edge (`public/assets/comps`). The app
  shows the bundled file first; it works offline and doesn't depend on any remote host.
- **Bundled, earlier art** (2): the Roshn Saudi League's RSL mark and the A-League Men mark, which football-logos.cc
  only has in older/other identities. Current, but 200px.
- **Edition logos** (3): the game's EURO is played in June 2028 (UK & Ireland) and its World Cup in June 2030
  (Spain, Portugal, Morocco), so those editions' own logos are bundled; the European Qualifiers and World Cup
  qualifiers of those cycles show them too. Later editions show the designed emblem until their logos exist.
- **Wikipedia on the device** (4): not on football-logos.cc, and Wikipedia has no usable logo for them (checked
  2026-10-21: no lead image, or a photo of the trophy). The device still looks, and uses an article's lead image only if
  it is a drawn logo (not a trophy photo) and, for a tournament, not one edition's; otherwise the designed emblem.
  The Trophée des Champions (Wikimedia Commons, 2024 logo) and the Africa Cup of Nations (its generic CAF/TotalEnergies
  logo on Wikipedia) were retrieved from Wikimedia and are bundled.
- **Designed emblem** (1): no authentic logo exists or none was found.

"Dark surface" is how the logo is shown on the app's dark cards (`scripts/assets/logo_treatments.py`): light (its
white version), reverse (dark tone white, colours kept), edge (its own rim lifted), or as it is (—). No logo is put on
a plate. Official white variants on football-logos.cc exist only at 256px palette quality, so the white versions are
made from the 700px masters.

Usage: football-logos.cc states the logos belong to their clubs, leagues and federations and may be used for
informational, editorial and non-commercial fan projects (https://football-logos.cc/license/). Touchline is a
non-commercial fan game; a commercial release would need licences from the rights holders.

## Competition identities

The biggest competitions have their own presentation on their page (title card), on the Season page tile and on the
Match Day band (`src/ui/compIdentity.ts`, styles under `.ci-<id>` in `src/styles/screens.css`).

- **Marks** (`public/assets/marks/*.svg`): the symbol of the genuine bundled logo, without its wordmark, traced into a
  single-colour vector by `scripts/assets/comp_marks.py` so it can be shown large and filled with the brand's colours:
  the starball, the Europa League trophy, the LALIGA "LL" (and Hypermotion), the Bundesliga and 2. Bundesliga players,
  Serie A's "A", the Ligue 1 and Ligue 2 symbols, the FA Cup trophy. The Premier League lion is the Simple Icons vector
  (from premierleague.com), not a trace.
- **Colours**: sampled from those logos where the app has them (UCL navy #00004b, Europa orange #ff6900, LALIGA coral
  #ff4b44, Bundesliga red #d20515, Serie A navy #1a1659 and azure #0070f8, Carabao green #008c5a), the Premier League's
  purple #37003c and accents, and otherwise taken from published descriptions of the identities with no official values
  found (Ligue 1's electric blue and pink on charcoal, Ligue 2's aquamarine and purple, the FA Cup's navy and red, the
  Champions League's "Kick of Light" spectrum, the Conference League's black and green). Those are approximations.
- Competitions without a bundled logo (Conference League, Copa del Rey, DFB-Pokal, Coppa Italia, Coupe de France, World
  Cup, EURO) get their colours and light but no mark, since their logos load only on the device and couldn't be traced or
  checked here.

## Player faces (checked 2026-10-21)

Retrieved and compared side by side for a sample across ratings and leagues (Haaland to League Two):

- **EA FC 27 Ultimate Team portraits** (EA's FUT Web App content store, `www.ea.com/.../content/<fut_guid>/2027/...`):
  this season's faces in this season's kits (Wirtz in Liverpool red, Gyökeres at Arsenal), 160×160. Coverage of the
  database: 100% of 80+ players, 97.5% of 60–79, 77.5% under 60 (none of the missing ones has an FC 26 face either).
  First choice for avatars (160px is native up to ~53 CSS px on a 3x screen).
- **FUTBIN / FUTWIZ**: byte-identical copies of the same FC 27 and FC 26 renders (160px, palette PNG): mirrors.
- **SoFIFA** (27_240): the same FC 27 render at 240px, first choice for big displays. Cloudflare answers the build
  environment with a bot block page (HTTP 403), so it could not be checked here; on a phone it is tried and dropped by
  the app if it keeps failing.
- **EA ratings site portraits** (`ratings-images-prod.pulse.ea.com/FC25/...`): real photos at 512px, but from 2024/25
  (Wirtz still at Leverkusen, Gyökeres at Sporting), so only a last fallback. Its CDN is Akamai Image Manager: the
  `width=` parameter the app used is ignored (365KB per face); `im=Resize,width=N` is the working resize. Its FC26/FC27
  paths don't exist (S3 403).

## Club crests (checked 2026-10-21)

All 473 bundled crests were compared with football-logos.cc's 512px art. 142 differed visibly: the bundled ones came
from older community logo snapshots (Montpellier's 50th-anniversary badge, Galatasaray with four stars, Beşiktaş
without its stars), and the sharper 512px art the app fades in on big displays was the current crest, so a club could
show two different crests. All 472 with current art were rebuilt from it (Vaduz has no current 512px art and keeps its
crest). Crests are transparent (none has a baked background), and each crest's margin was measured so every crest
fills its box the same way (`src/data/crestFit.json`).

## Manager portraits (checked 2026-10-21)

Managers are shown from their Wikipedia article's lead image on the device (`services/assets.ts managerWikiQuery`),
accepted only when the article is about the same person (a football manager or coach, with their nationality or one of
their clubs named; `scripts/qa/manager-photos.ts` tests that check). Sixteen were retrieved and looked at, from Arteta
and Guardiola down to the second tiers: every one was the right person and current enough. Some lead images are wide
shots (on a touchline, at a press table) where the face is small; the app fills its square avatar from them, held at 20% from the
top of the frame (`.avatar img`, where a head usually is), and nothing was guessed beyond that: there is no licensed source of tight, current manager headshots, so those
stay as Wikipedia has them. Managers without an article, or whose article fails the check, show initials.

## Seeing remote images from the build environment

With full network access (2026-10-21) every image host is reachable through the environment's proxy, with three
exceptions that are the providers' own decisions, not the environment's: SoFIFA (Cloudflare bot block, HTTP 403),
Wikimedia (`en.wikipedia.org`, `commons.wikimedia.org`, `upload.wikimedia.org`: HTTP 429 on bursts of requests even
with a policy-compliant User-Agent; a slow queue that honours `Retry-After` gets through, which is how the Trophée des
Champions and Africa Cup of Nations logos and the Wikipedia checks above were done), and EA's FC26/FC27 ratings-portrait paths (S3 403: they don't exist). The headless
browser needs the proxy passed to it (`scripts/qa/load-save.mjs` does) and the proxy's CA in its NSS store
(`certutil -d sql:$HOME/.pki/nssdb -A -t "C,," -n proxy-ca -i <ca.pem>`).
